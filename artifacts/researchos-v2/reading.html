4.2  Low-Step Sampling Regime

Empirical comparison across parameterizations

Table 2 reports FID-50k under the reduced step budget. We hold the denoiser, the schedule and the solver order fixed, varying only the reparameterization ψ, so that differences isolate the effect of the noise-injection term.

We observe that the deterministic (σ→0) limit attains lower FID than stochastic sampling for step counts between 10 and 201, which we initially attribute to the accumulated discretization error of the stochastic trajectory.

This reading, however, presumes a common error model across solvers, an assumption we revisit in Appendix B. Below 10 steps the ordering reverses for all ψ in our family.

Consistent with the churn analysis, the reparameterized sampler injects noise once per step, whereas EDM adds one extra stochastic round-trip2. The two are therefore not algebraically equivalent, a point we state explicitly.

Error bars in Table 2 denote one standard deviation over three seeds; the reported gaps are smaller than twice that deviation for N ≥ 20.

Absolute FIDs are not comparable across datasets, and we therefore report relative deltas against the DDIM baseline in the right column.
